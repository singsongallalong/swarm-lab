# A: glacier tongue over a granite ledge

Keep: the ice-cave sculpt itself (a dark mouth under a slab lintel) is the one piece with real character.

1. Give the shot a subject. In every "tongue over the cave mouth" and game-camera shot the serac field is a carpet of near-identical pale boxes with no silhouette; the cave mouth is lost. Make one big pour (a few tall seracs dropping in clear steps toward the cave) and keep the rest of the ledge bare dark granite so the ice reads against it.
2. Move it off the north wall. All twelve seeds park the tongue on the top edge with the road looping below it; only the road's kinks change. Let the pour come from different edges and let the road pass under, beside or across its foot.
3. The meltwater set piece is a faint scatter of drips with a dot; at game camera it is almost invisible. Make the drip a readable sheet or column falling into a pool that glows, so the cave has a reason to be looked at.
