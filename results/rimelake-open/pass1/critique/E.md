# E: notes for the rework

Keep: "the nave", the road running down an avenue of towering pillars to a far opening. It is the strongest frame in the run: it has scale, a vanishing point and a real sense of place.

1. Give the nave a subject at its end. The far opening shows only more pillars. Put a single dark arch, altar or barrow mound where the road vanishes, and light the barrow wisps rising from it. Then the photo has an eye-line and a reason to walk.
2. "The stones" is filled by dark slabs a few studs away, and "from the highland" is a flat wall of pillars at the horizon. Move the stones camera out to the edge of the pillar forest, about 20 cells back. Look in along one aisle. Lift the highland camera about 15 and tilt down so the forest reads as a field of spires with the oval rim behind it.
3. The basin is still an oval bowl with a band of ground around it, and the three seeds are near-identical. Break the rim on one side into a drop or a frozen shore. Vary the road: one seed straight down the nave, one dog-legging through a clearing. Clear two or three open courts in the pillar forest, wide enough to fight a pack in.
