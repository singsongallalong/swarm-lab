# A: notes for the rework

Keep: the terraced switchback descending from the north shelf onto the lake plateau. It is the one shape here that reads as a real climb down.

1. None of your three shots has a subject. "The frozen falls" and "the runestone ring" both look flat across empty ice at a far skyline of grey slabs. "Down the cascade" is filled by one pale block a few studs from the lens. Stand the cascade camera at the top of the switchback, about 20 cells back from the edge and lifted 15. Look down the terraces to the lake with the runestone ring in the lower third, so the descent itself becomes the picture.
2. The falls have no height you can see. The north shelf drops onto the lake in low steps. Make the last drop one sheer face 3–4 times a hero's height, with the ice cascade spilling down it, and put the falls shot at its foot looking up.
3. The three seeds are the same map. Let the switchback swap sides or vary its number of turns, and move the ring across the lake from seed to seed. The under-ice streams show only as one thin line. Run several braided channels under the open ice where the road crosses so the player walks over them.


Across the whole run, the critic also noted: No candidate has a store-page photo: every set has at least one camera jammed against a slab, and the seeds of each map barely differ. Four of six reuse the same faint under-ice line as their effect, and nobody built a single tall subject (a tower or arch) that the best shot is framed around.
