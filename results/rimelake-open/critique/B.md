# B: notes for the rework

Keep: the barrow wisps. They're the only effect in the run that reads from the game camera, and they look ghostly and alive. Keep them pale, and keep them rising out of the ground.

1. Every shot is blocked by a pillar. "The dais", "the needle" and the eye-level road views each put a tall slab 5 to 15 studs from the lens, filling a third to half of the frame. Pull the cameras back 30 or more cells and up 10 to 15, so the needles stand in the middle ground against the skyline with the lake in front. In the frame, a needle should be a subject, not a wall.
2. From above, it's still the moor: a lumpy bowl, a band of ground round a central pool, props sprinkled across it. Give the ground a shape of its own. Drop the lake well below the band, cut one or two deep channels or causeways across it, and let the needle forest grow from one side only, so the map has a direction.
3. Put the wisps where the road goes, not on their own. Rise them out of the barrows on the dais, in the frame of your best "from the highland" shot, so the effect is part of the photograph and marks the landmark. The three seeds also look nearly the same: move the dais and the lake's shape with the seed.
