# D: tiered salt skirt, driftwood quill crest

**Most important fix: soften the stepped "wedding cake".** The three hard tiers, each a flat-topped ring wider than the last, are the strongest dress silhouette in the field, but from the top-down camera the flat ledges read as a plinth or pagoda, and the bottom tier sits on the floor like a pedestal. Raise the hem to the shins and make each tier slope or overlap the next (a draped flounce), so it reads as fabric, not masonry.

What works:
- Reads instantly as a gown at ARPG pitch: a narrow top over a big flared base. No other family looks like this.
- The palette is on brief: salt/ivory body, driftwood-brown bands, thin teal edge on each tier. It is muted and aged.
- The driftwood quill crest is a nice salvage touch and stands clear of the head.
- The skirt is continuous on both avatars with no gaps; the tiers hand over cleanly.
- The back X lacing is a good touch on the darker back.

What to change:
- The torso is a wide box with heavy layered sleeves. The shoulders are almost as wide as the top tier, which kills the fitted-waist read. Narrow the shoulders and sleeves and pull the waist in under the teal sash.
- The brown chest plates are fussy detail that is lost from above. Spend those parts on the tier edges (shell or brass trim).
- The back is a uniform darker grey. Echo the tiers' ivory there too so the 360 view stays a gown.
