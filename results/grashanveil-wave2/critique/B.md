# B: ziggurat crown, stepped temple gown

**1. Cut the sleeves down (most important).** The sleeves are stacks of horizontal slabs, wider than the shoulders, and they continue the stepped skirt straight up the body. The result is a walking ziggurat: the widest point is at the shoulders and elbows, the opposite of a feminine figure. Use narrow single-column sleeves in a quieter tone so the pale stepped mass starts at the hip.

**2. Taper the torso.** Above the waist the figure is one big rectangle. Pull in the bodice and add a clear waist band so the step-out happens below it.

**3. The skirt flares but reads as a plinth.** The terracotta-edged tiers do widen toward the hem, and from above this is the most "carved ruin stone" silhouette of the six, which is a real strength for the zone. But it reads as architecture and not cloth. Use fewer tiers (3, not 5 or more), make each one taller and make the steps wider apart. You keep the temple idea and gain a bell.

4. The stepped crown is excellent: strong height at the head and very much Grashan. Keep it and add a sun-gold finial.

5. The colour is almost all one stone grey. Add a sun-gold accent band on the skirt so it is not mistaken for a statue in the night palette.
